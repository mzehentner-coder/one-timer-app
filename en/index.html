What makes One Timer different

One tap is enough

45 time tiles from 1 minute to 3 hours. Tap a time and the timer runs. No setting, no confirming, no start button.

Several timers

Up to four run at once. Tap the same tile twice for two separate timers.

A reliable alarm

Rings with the phone locked, in silent mode and after a restart. Full screen over the lock screen, and it can announce which timer is up.

Nothing to clean up

Finished timers disappear on their own. Several finished timers make one alarm with one stop button.

Your times, your names

Long-press a tile to change or name it: “Laundry”, “Parking”, “Break”. Odd times like 7:30 with a single swipe.

No ads, no account, no internet

One Timer has no internet access and collects no data. Eight designs, eight languages, a home screen widget.

How it looks

If an alarm does not ring on time

Some manufacturers stop background apps to save battery. An alarm may then fail to ring with the screen locked. One Timer shows under + → Settings → Reliability whether everything is set and takes you to the right place. Depending on your phone, you may also need:

  • Samsung: Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → add One Timer to “Never sleeping apps”.
  • Xiaomi: In App info, turn on “Autostart” and set “Battery saver” to “No restrictions”.
  • OnePlus/Oppo: In App info under “Battery”, allow background activity and turn on “Auto launch”.
  • Huawei/Honor: Under “App launch”, set One Timer to manual and turn on all three switches.
  • Vivo: Under “Battery” → “High background power consumption”, allow One Timer.
